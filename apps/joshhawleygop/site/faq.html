Josh Hawley FAQ: the short answers.

The questions people search most about Missouri’s junior senator, answered in a few sentences each, with links to the full, sourced story.

Where does Josh Hawley live?

In January 2019, weeks after being sworn in, Hawley bought a five-bedroom house in Vienna, Virginia, a Washington suburb now valued at around $1.7 million. That spring he sold his Columbia, Missouri home and registered to vote at his sister’s house in Ozark. He now owns two properties in Christian County, Missouri, acquired in 2021 and late 2023. Read the full residency story.

Did Josh Hawley vote to cut Medicaid?

Yes. On July 1, 2025 he voted for the One Big Beautiful Bill, which includes hundreds of billions of dollars in Medicaid cuts; it passed the Senate 51–50. Weeks earlier he had written that cutting Medicaid was “morally wrong,” and two weeks after the vote he filed a bill to undo some of the cuts, which hasn’t moved since. See the full Medicaid timeline.

What did Josh Hawley do on January 6?

He was the first senator to announce he’d object to certifying the 2020 election, and he objected to Pennsylvania’s electoral votes (the objection failed 92 to 7). That morning he raised a fist to the crowd outside the Capitol; hours later, security footage showed him running from the mob. His campaign later sold mugs with the fist-pump photo. Read what happened on January 6.

What is Josh Hawley’s net worth?

There’s no exact figure: senators report assets and debts only in broad value ranges on annual financial disclosures, so estimates vary widely. Estimates built from his disclosures range from a few hundred thousand dollars to a few million. His best-known assets are the Vienna, Virginia house and income from his books. You can read his filings yourself in the Senate’s financial disclosure database, or see an estimate at Quiver Quantitative.

Is Josh Hawley from Missouri?

He was born in Springdale, Arkansas on December 31, 1979, and grew up in Lexington, Missouri. Since joining the Senate in 2019, his family’s main home has been in Vienna, Virginia. See his official congressional biography and Wikipedia entry.

How old is Josh Hawley?

Josh Hawley was born on December 31, 1979, which made him 46 years old as of September 2026. (Congressional biography, Wikipedia.)

When is Josh Hawley up for re-election?

Not until November 2030. He won a second term in November 2024 with 55.6% of the vote against Marine veteran Lucas Kunce, and that term runs until January 2031. (Missouri Independent.)

Is Josh Hawley running for president in 2028?

He says no. Asked in September 2026, he told the Washington Examiner: “No, I have no intention of doing so, period.” (Political Wire.)

Where did Josh Hawley go to school?

He graduated from Stanford University and Yale Law School, then clerked for Chief Justice John Roberts before becoming Missouri’s attorney general in January 2017. (Congressional biography, Wikipedia.) Ten months into that job, he was running for the Senate.

Did Josh Hawley object to the 2020 election results?

Yes. He was the first senator to say he would object, and on January 6, 2021 he joined the objection to Pennsylvania’s electoral votes, one of only seven senators to vote for it. What Hawley did on January 6.

Did Josh Hawley vote for the PACT Act?

Eventually. He voted for the burn-pit bill in June 2022, switched to no in July with a Republican bloc that stalled it, then flipped back to yes in August after veterans protested on the Capitol steps. Read about the flip-flop.

What is Josh Hawley’s position on abortion and Amendment 3?

He opposed the 2024 Amendment 3 that Missouri voters passed with 51.7%, and he’s campaigning for the 2026 Amendment 3, which would replace it with a near-total ban. In September 2026 he also launched a national push to ban the abortion pill mifepristone. Hawley’s push to overturn the 2024 vote.

What did Josh Hawley say about Ketanji Brown Jackson?

He claimed Judge Jackson had a “pattern” of going soft on child pornography offenders. PolitiFact found he had cherry-picked her record: her sentences matched national practice, and Hawley had voted to confirm Trump judges who handed down similar ones. Read the fact-check story.
